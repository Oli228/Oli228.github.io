Elinor Ostrom[^1]
a Nobel Prize-winning political economist whose groundbreaking research provided the intellectual foundation for modern, community-based conservation policies used in parks and protected areas worldwide

She identified numerous cases where local communities had developed their own complex and effective rules for sustainably managing resources over centuries.

“Governing the Commons”

  1. Clearly Defined Boundaries: The community knows who has rights to the resource.
  2. Rules Matched to Local Needs: Regulations are adapted to the local environment and social conditions.
  3. Collective-Choice Arrangements: Those affected by the rules can participate in modifying them.
  4. Effective Monitoring: The community itself monitors the state of the resource and user behavior.
  5. Graduated Sanctions: Rule-breakers face penalties that start small and escalate.
  6. Conflict-Resolution Mechanisms: There are accessible, low-cost ways to resolve disputes.
  7. Minimal Recognition of Rights by Government: The government respects the community’s self-governance.
